If you plan to stay in Korea for more than 90 days, you must register as a foreign resident and get a registration card, commonly called the ARC (Alien Registration Card; 외국인등록증). Almost everything in Korean daily life, from a phone plan to a bank account, gets easier once you have it.

Who needs one, and by when?

  • Foreigners staying in Korea longer than 90 days must register.
  • The deadline is within 90 days of entering Korea. Don't leave it to the last week: appointments can be booked out.
  • Many long-term visa holders (for example work, study and family visas) register this way. Short-term visitors and tourists don't.

Step 1: Book an appointment on HiKorea

Registration is done at the immigration office (출입국·외국인청 or 출장소) that covers the area where you live. Book a visit on HiKorea, the government's immigration portal: choose the office for your address and the task “Foreign resident registration”. Walk-ins are often turned away at busy offices.

Some universities and large employers book group appointments for their students and staff, so ask them first.

Step 2: Prepare your documents

Requirements depend on your visa, so check the list for your visa type on HiKorea. Usually you'll need:

  • Your passport
  • The application form (available at the office or on HiKorea)
  • One color photo, 3.5 × 4.5 cm, taken within the last 6 months
  • The fee, paid at the office (check the current amount on HiKorea)
  • Proof of address, such as your lease contract or a dormitory certificate
  • Visa-specific papers: for example an employment contract or business registration of your employer (work visas), a certificate of enrollment (students), or a marriage certificate (spouse visas)

Bring copies and a pen, and have your Korean address in both Korean and English. The Korean address converter helps with the English version.

Step 3: At the immigration office

Arrive a little early, take a number, and hand over your documents. Your fingerprints and photo are taken at the counter. Staff usually speak some English, and larger offices have interpreters for common languages.

Step 4: Wait for the card

Processing typically takes a few weeks. Depending on the office, you collect the card in person or receive it by mail. You can check progress on HiKorea. Keep your receipt: it shows you applied on time.

After you get your ARC

  • Report address changes quickly. When you move, report your new address to your local community service center (주민센터) or online, within the deadline in the Immigration Act (15 days). Late reports can lead to a fine.
  • Extend before it expires. The card's expiry follows your permitted stay. Apply for an extension on HiKorea before the date, not after.
  • Carry it. Foreign residents are expected to carry their registration card or passport.
  • Next steps: with your ARC you can open a full bank account, sign a postpaid phone plan and join health insurance. See how to open a bank account and health insurance for foreigners.

Your name on the card

Your name on the ARC is copied from your passport. Use exactly that spelling on every Korean form, account and contract to avoid problems later. If Korean friends need to write your name in Hangul, the spelling is usually done by sound.

Procedures and fees change. Always check the current requirements for your visa on HiKorea or call the Immigration Contact Center at 1345 (multilingual).
