Most prices in Korea include 10% VAT. Visitors who take goods home can get part of it back. Here's how it works, and what to check before you head to the airport.

Who can claim?

  • Foreign visitors staying in Korea for less than 6 months.
  • Generally not foreigners who live or work in Korea (for example, people staying 6 months or more or earning income here), and not diplomats or US forces personnel stationed in Korea.
  • Overseas Koreans living abroad may qualify under special conditions.

So if you have a work visa and an ARC, you usually can't use tax refund, but your visiting family and friends can.

Where to shop

Look for a “Tax Free” or “Tax Refund” sign at the entrance or counter. Department stores, cosmetics chains, large brand stores and many shops in tourist areas take part. Duty-free shops (where you buy before your flight) are a different system.

How much do you need to spend?

At the time of writing, most tax-free shops accept refunds from about ₩15,000 per receipt, though some stores set a higher minimum. Ask at the counter. You get back roughly 5–8% of the price after the refund operator's fee, not the full 10%.

Option 1: instant refund in the store

Many shops offer an immediate refund (즉시환급): show your passport at the till and the tax is taken off on the spot. There are limits per purchase and per trip, and the store records the purchase on your passport. You still need to take the goods out of Korea.

Option 2: refund at the airport or port

  1. At the shop, show your passport and ask for a tax refund receipt. Keep it with the original receipt.
  2. Leave Korea within 3 months of the purchase, with the goods.
  3. At the airport, go to a tax refund kiosk before check-in if the goods will go in your checked bag. Scan your passport and receipts. If customs wants to see the items, show them at the customs desk.
  4. Receive your refund at the kiosk or refund counter, in cash or to a card depending on the operator. At major airports such as Incheon and Gimpo, there are also refund counters after security for items in hand luggage.

Some city centers also have downtown refund counters where you can get money back before you go to the airport. You still need to confirm the export when you leave.

Tips

  • Keep goods unused and in original packaging in case customs asks to see them.
  • Food and restaurant meals usually aren't refundable.
  • Allow extra time at the airport: kiosk lines can be long in peak season.
  • Rules and limits change. Check the latest at the refund counter or with the Korea Tourism Organization's 1330 Travel Hotline (multilingual).
