Korea's National Health Insurance (NHI, run by the National Health Insurance Service, 국민건강보험공단) covers almost everyone living in Korea, including most foreign residents. Joining is mostly automatic, but the rules for foreigners have a few twists worth knowing.

The 6-month rule

Since 2019, foreign residents who have stayed in Korea for 6 months or more are automatically enrolled as regional subscribers if they aren't covered through a job. Some visa types, such as students and marriage migrants, join from the date they register. You don't need to apply: NHIS sends a notice and the first bill to your registered address. Make sure your address is up to date.

How much do regional subscribers pay?

Premiums for regional subscribers are based on income and property in Korea. For foreigners there is a minimum premium, set close to the average premium of all subscribers, so many foreign regional subscribers pay that minimum even with little income. Students on study visas get a large discount. The exact amount changes every year; your bill from NHIS shows it.

Family members (dependents)

  • A workplace subscriber's spouse and children under 19 can usually be added as dependents (피부양자) without paying extra.
  • Since April 2024, other foreign family members (such as parents) must also have lived in Korea for 6 months before they can be added as dependents.
  • Dependents must meet income and property limits.

What it covers

NHI covers most doctor visits, hospital stays, prescriptions, dental basics and health checkups. You pay part of the bill yourself: typically about 20% for hospital stays and around 30–60% for outpatient visits, depending on the size of the hospital. Some treatments, such as many cosmetic procedures, aren't covered. Show your registration card at the reception desk so the insurance is applied.

NHIS also offers free general health checkups every two years for adult subscribers. Watch for the letter or app notification.

Don't let premiums go unpaid

Unpaid premiums have real consequences for foreigners:

  • You may have to pay the full hospital bill yourself while the arrears remain.
  • Immigration can restrict visa extensions and other stay permits until the arrears are paid.

If you leave Korea for good, let NHIS know (or your departure will be recorded automatically) and settle the final bill.

Can I opt out?

Generally no. Foreign residents can be excluded from regional enrollment only in limited cases, such as having equivalent coverage under a foreign employer's plan or a social security agreement. Check with NHIS before assuming you're exempt.

Getting help in English

NHIS runs a foreigner helpline with English and other languages: call 1577-1000 and choose the foreign language option. You can also ask at any NHIS branch, which handles bills, certificates and address updates.

Rules and premiums change. This guide reflects publicly announced rules as of November 2026; check with NHIS for your own situation.
