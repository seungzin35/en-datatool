A Korean bank account is the key to getting paid, paying rent and using local apps. Opening one is simple once you know what the bank will ask for, and why your new account might have a low limit at first.

What you need

  • Alien Registration Card (ARC): most banks require it for a regular account. See how to get your ARC.
  • Passport
  • A Korean phone number, ideally in your own name, for verification texts and app sign-up
  • Proof of why you need the account: an employment contract or certificate of employment (재직증명서), a certificate of enrollment for students, or similar

Some branches can open an account with a passport only before you get your ARC, but these accounts usually come with strict limits and fewer services. Policies differ by bank and even by branch, so call ahead or go to a branch near a university or industrial area that often serves foreigners.

Why your new account has a low limit

To fight phone scams and illegal account trading, Korean banks open most new accounts as limited accounts (한도제한계좌). You can receive money freely, but daily withdrawals and transfers are capped at a low amount until you show the account is for a genuine purpose.

How to lift it: bring proof such as a certificate of employment, payslips showing salary into the account, or a lease contract, and ask the bank to lift the limit (한도 해제). After a few months of regular salary deposits, many banks lift it on request.

At the branch

  1. Take a number for “individual banking” (개인) and show your ARC and passport.
  2. Fill in the forms. Staff may help in English; some banks have foreigner-dedicated branches.
  3. Ask for a debit card (체크카드) linked to the account. It works in shops, online and at ATMs.
  4. Ask staff to help you set up mobile banking on your phone before you leave. Verification can be tricky the first time.

Mobile banking and payments

Korean banking apps let you transfer money instantly to any Korean account for free or a small fee. Once your account is set up you can also register it in payment apps such as Kakao Pay, Naver Pay or Toss for quick payments and splitting bills.

Getting paid and paying rent

  • Give your employer the bank name and account number (계좌번호) for salary.
  • Rent and maintenance fees are usually paid by bank transfer to the landlord. Always transfer to the account in the landlord's own name. See how to read Korean rent listings.

Sending money home

Banks offer international transfers (해외송금), and many foreigners also use licensed remittance apps that are often cheaper. Large or frequent transfers may require you to designate one bank for overseas remittance and show proof of income, such as your payslips or tax documents.

Before you leave Korea

Close accounts you won't use and move your money out first. An unused account can be hard to access from abroad once your ARC expires.
