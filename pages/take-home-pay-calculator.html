What gets deducted from a Korean salary

Korean payslips (급여명세서) usually show six deductions. Four are social insurance (4대보험) and two are taxes.

Your employer pays the same amount again for pension, health, long-term care and employment insurance, plus all of the industrial accident insurance (산재보험). Those employer payments do not come out of your salary.

How this calculator works

  • Insurance base: monthly salary minus tax-free allowances.
  • National Pension is 4.75% of that base, but the base is capped. From July 2026 to June 2027 the base is at least ₩410,000 and at most ₩6,590,000 a month, so the most you pay is ₩313,020.
  • Long-term care = health insurance premium × 0.9448 ÷ 7.19.
  • Amounts under 10 won are dropped, as on real payslips.
  • Income tax is worked out for the whole year: earned income deduction, ₩1,500,000 per person you support, your pension and insurance premiums, the 2026 tax brackets, the earned income tax credit and the ₩130,000 standard tax credit. The result is divided by 12. Child tax credits and other personal deductions are not included.

Notes for foreign workers

  • National Pension: whether you pay depends on your country's agreement with Korea and your visa. Some people can claim a lump-sum refund (반환일시금) when they leave Korea. Check with the National Pension Service.
  • Employment Insurance: mandatory for some visas (for example F-2, F-5, F-6, E-9) and optional for many others.
  • Flat tax option: some foreign employees can choose a flat income tax rate instead of the regular progressive rate. This calculator uses the regular rate. Ask your employer if the flat rate applies to you.
