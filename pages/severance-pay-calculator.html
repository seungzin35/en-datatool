Who gets severance pay in Korea?

Under the Employee Retirement Benefit Security Act, an employee gets severance pay if they:

  • worked for the same employer for at least one year (365 days), and
  • worked on average 15 hours or more a week.

This applies to foreign workers as well, including part-time workers who meet both conditions. Your visa type and nationality don't matter.

How severance is calculated

Average daily wage = (pay in the last 3 months + 3/12 of bonuses + 3/12 of leave pay) ÷ number of calendar days in those 3 months Severance = average daily wage × 30 × (days employed ÷ 365)

In short: about one month's average pay for every year worked, counted to the day. Two and a half years of work gives about two and a half months' pay.

When must it be paid?

Your employer must pay severance within 14 days of your last day, unless you both agree to extend that. In many cases it is paid into an IRP retirement account (개인형 퇴직연금) in your name rather than your normal bank account, and you can then cancel the IRP to receive the money. If you are leaving Korea, tell your employer early and ask how it will be paid.

Workers on E-9 and H-2 visas

Employers of E-9 (non-professional employment) and H-2 (working visit) workers usually pay part of severance through departure guarantee insurance (출국만기보험), which you claim when you leave Korea. If the insurance pays less than your legal severance, the employer owes the difference.

If your employer doesn't pay

You can file a complaint with the Ministry of Employment and Labor (call 1350, with interpretation available). Keep your contract, payslips and bank records.
