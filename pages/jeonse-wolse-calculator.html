How the conversion works

Monthly rent = (jeonse deposit − new deposit) × conversion rate ÷ 12 Jeonse equivalent = deposit + monthly rent × 12 ÷ conversion rate

The conversion rate (전월세 전환율) is the yearly interest the landlord expects on the deposit they no longer hold. A higher rate means higher monthly rent for the same reduction in deposit.

Which rate should I use?

  • Market rate: what landlords in the area actually ask. Compare two listings for similar homes, one jeonse and one wolse, to estimate it. The calculator's 5% is only a starting example.
  • Legal cap during a contract: when an existing jeonse is converted to wolse during the contract or on renewal, the Housing Lease Protection Act caps the rate at the Bank of Korea base rate plus 2 percentage points. New contracts are negotiated freely.

Jeonse or wolse: which is better for you?

Compare the monthly rent with what the deposit money would cost you otherwise. If you would borrow the deposit at 4% a year, a ₩100,000,000 deposit costs about ₩333,000 a month in interest. If the landlord asks more than that in rent instead, jeonse is cheaper, but you carry the risk of getting a big deposit back. Read how to read Korean rent listings for the safety checks.
