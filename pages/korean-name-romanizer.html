How Korean names are romanized

Korea's official system is the Revised Romanization of Korean (2000). For personal names it has three simple rules:

  • The family name comes first and is written separately: 한복남 → Han Boknam.
  • The given name is written as one word. A hyphen between syllables is allowed: Han Bok-nam.
  • Sound changes between the syllables of a given name are not written. 복남 sounds like “bong-nam” but is written Boknam.

Why family names look different

Family names are the big exception. Long before the official system, people chose their own spellings, and those stuck. Under the official rules 김 is “Gim”, 이 is “I” and 박 is “Bak”, but almost everyone writes Kim, Lee and Park. Both are correct; what matters is using one spelling consistently.

Passports, visas and bank accounts

  • The English name in a Korean passport is chosen when the first passport is issued, and it is hard to change later. Keep the same spelling on flight tickets, visas, bank accounts and diplomas.
  • If you are filling in a form for a Korean friend or colleague, ask how their name is written in their passport instead of guessing.
  • For your own non-Korean name in Korea, use the spelling exactly as it appears on your passport or Alien Registration Card.
